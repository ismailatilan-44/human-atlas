# Whole-skull learning goals and content options

Research checked **2026-10-02**. This is original synthesis of the cited sources. The sequence, target counts, and prioritization below are **proposed**, not a validated curriculum, implementation choice, or expert-accepted answer key. Current fine-feature geometry and educational efficacy remain unestablished.

## Preserve the concept distinctions

The nominal adult count is **22 bones: eight cranial and fourteen facial**, or fourteen types when paired instances are grouped. This anatomical convention is separate from a manifest membership audit.

Use a navigable graph rather than forcing all concepts into a single-parent tree:

- Bone identity has side, paired/unpaired status, classification, and terminology.
- A surface feature belongs to a bone; a suture relates participating bones.
- A composite structure has multiple contributors; a cavity or region has boundaries.
- An opening has bony boundaries; a canal also has a course and endpoints.
- A nerve or vessel is related to a passage, not identical to it.

Keep source mesh IDs, anatomical identities, visible features, and educational targets distinct. **Present in geometry**, **separately selectable**, **labelled**, and **anatomically verified** are independent claims.

Cranial bones are not synonymous with calvaria. Frontal, temporal, and occipital bones contribute beyond the skullcap; hiding entire cranial bones cannot automatically be called calvarial removal. Bone classification also differs from a bone's contribution to the face, orbit, or nasal cavity. [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-2-the-skull)

## Proposed progression

| Stage | Learning goal and task examples | Evidence to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — orientation | Establish anatomical directions and specimen right/left; distinguish external/intracranial view; relocate an isolated bone in the assembled skull. | Correct side and orientation from unfamiliar views, without fixed colors. Separate orientation errors from naming errors. |
| B — all bone identities | Find a named bone; name an indicated bone; classify cranial/facial and paired/unpaired; find the other side; reveal small obscured bones. | All fourteen types and relevant laterality; both assembled and isolated recognition. Small facial bones remain actual targets. |
| C — features and parent bones | Identify a process, ridge, plate, or depression, then its parent; distinguish nearby alternatives and locate the feature from another view. | Correct feature and bone, distinguishable distractor, and a defensible visible target rather than a nearby floating label. |
| D — boundaries and composites | Relate sutures to participating bones; identify contributors to arches, palate, septum, articulation, orbit, and nasal cavity. | Correct contributors and relationship; isolated-bone recognition alone does not complete the task. Explicit bilateral handling. |
| E — external skull and cranial floor | Locate three fossae and their boundaries; relocate a bone/opening between internal and external views; explain what a removal or section exposed. | Correct region/boundary and distinction between space and floor-forming bone. Never require guessing missing or obscured detail. |
| F — passages and destinations | Locate an opening; identify its bony boundaries, connected regions, and selected principal contents; find it from the opposite relevant view. | Spatial relationship beyond an opening-to-nerve name pair. Limited contents are labelled as such. |
| G — representation transfer | Recognize learned structures in a second skull photograph, sectional illustration, or verified reference imaging. | Recognition independent of the initial rendering. Requires separately licensed/reviewed assets. |

Stage C contrast candidates include mandibular condylar versus coronoid processes and temporal mastoid versus styloid processes. [TTUHSC infratemporal/oral lab](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/nervous_system/infratemp.html)

Stage D early candidates: coronal, sagittal, lambdoid, and squamous suture families; zygomatic arch; hard palate; bony nasal septum; bony temporomandibular articulation; orbit and nasal cavity. These are **proposed teaching targets**, not independently confirmed Atlas representations.

Stage E candidates include crista galli, sphenoid wings, sella region, petrous ridge, clivus, and foramen magnum. [TTUHSC cranial cavity lab](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/schemes/brain_intro.html)

Stage F proposes twelve passage families: cribriform foramina, optic canal, superior orbital fissure, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale, foramen spinosum, carotid canal, internal acoustic meatus, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, foramen magnum, and stylomastoid foramen. This list is **not** a finding that the current meshes adequately represent them. Record “principal contents taught here,” not “all contents,” for bounded content. Institutional orientation reference: [Utah Digital Dissector skull foramina](https://anatomy.med.utah.edu/diganat/anatomy_tutorials/digital_dissector/index.php?add=%2Fforamen&lab=18&menu=18.1+Skull+Foramina&selectsize=Medium&textsize=Large).

A clipped polygon model is not a CT image or proof of tissue appearance, attenuation, or real sectional anatomy. Stage G requires its own evidence. [Loyola objectives](https://www.meddean.luc.edu/lumen/meded/grossanatomy/h_n/hn_obj.html)

## Anticipated misconceptions to test

These are design risks, not measured prevalence claims:

- Cranial bones equal calvaria; any bone visible in the face is a facial bone.
- A named process or plate is an additional bone.
- Every opening belongs to one bone; an arch is identical to the bone sharing its name.
- A canal, its opening, and its contents are interchangeable.
- Related internal/external openings necessarily form a straight tunnel.
- Every dry-skull gap is unobstructed in life.
- Hiding a mesh demonstrates anatomical absence.
- Fixed color or camera position is an anatomical identifying property.

Useful corrective contrasts include superior/middle nasal conchae as ethmoid features versus the separate inferior nasal concha; temporal and occipital boundaries of jugular foramen; mandibular coronoid versus condylar processes; and external versus internal acoustic meatus without suggesting a simple connecting open tube. Use independently checked answer keys. [TTUHSC head-and-neck bone table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/bones_head_neck.html)

## Representation requirements before choosing mechanisms

| Target | Required evidence or representation | Insufficient substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole bone | Separate source-preserved mesh with verified identity/context | A source group merely named for the bone |
| Surface feature | Anatomically checked anchor or region | Whole-bone highlight presented as feature segmentation |
| Suture | Boundary-specific representation and both participants | One bone highlighted without the shared boundary |
| Cranial fossa | Regional boundaries and useful internal view | A single point treated as the whole region |
| Foramen | Reviewed opening target and bony context | A label beside an unverified depression/gap |
| Canal | Demonstrable course/endpoints, or explicitly schematic reference | An opening treated as proof of the complete tunnel |
| Nerve/vessel content | Sourced name and regional connection; separately validated content for realistic course | Proximity-derived course or invented connection |
| Sinus/thin plate | Demonstrable surfaces, validated volume, or reviewed reference image | Clip plane assumed to supply missing walls |
| Calvarial removal | Defensible section/removal representation | Hiding all whole cranial bones |

A label cannot repair missing geometry. Cutting, transparency, and animation reveal or transform available representation; none establishes absent anatomy.

## Bounded content options — no product decision yet

| Option | Proposed scope | Value and gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — whole-skull orientation | All 22 instances; orientation; cranial/facial, paired/unpaired; assembled/isolated recognition; neighboring bones | Closest to existing separate-bone organization, subject to inventory verification. Does not complete landmarks, sutures, or passages. |
| 2 — relational core | Option 1; four suture families; eight target families (mastoid, styloid, occipital condyles, mandibular condylar and coronoid processes, zygomatic arches, hard palate, bony nasal septum); three fossae and principal boundaries; contributor questions | Research recommendation for an initial content target, conditional on each target being visible and independently reviewed. **Not user-selected.** |
| 3 — core plus passages | Option 2; twelve passage families; selected principal contents, bone boundaries, destinations, cross-view/reference transfer | Gated extension requiring more anatomy and geometry/reference validation. **Not user-selected.** |

Keep the whole skull in scope for every option. Initially defer exhaustive neurovascular contents, pterygopalatine networks, detailed temporal canals, neonatal/developmental anatomy, variation, complete muscle attachments, and clinical/surgical localization claims. Deferral is a proposed staging choice, not deletion from the wider atlas scope.

## Assessment and instructional release

Track identification, localization in changed views, relationships, and transfer separately. Record hints and answer type. A naming score must not imply canal-course understanding. Passing thresholds require the intended instructor; no percentage cutoff is validated here.

For each authored target, review names/synonyms, side, parent/contributor identities, evidence source, anchor/region in at least two useful views, multi-bone boundaries, question visibility, selected contents and endpoint wording, explicit schematic inference, unambiguous answers/distractors, and rights for every reused item. Anatomy expert acceptance remains pending; organized content and prototypes are not expert-validated medical instruction.

## Contradictions, terminology, and reuse

The following were checked in the supplied research on **2026-10-02**. They are source-specific cautions, not permission to import content.

- Charles University's pterion sentence omits temporal bone. Retain its assessment approach, not that sentence as an authoritative answer key. [Source](https://anatomie.lf2.cuni.cz/en/skull)
- OpenStax's absolute wording about nothing passing through foramen lacerum is unsuitable for a rigorous item. A primary anatomical study distinguishes fibrocartilage occupying that region from the internal carotid artery above it. A bounded formulation separates the ICA-transmitting carotid canal from the subsequent course above foramen lacerum. Detailed lacerum assessment is outside the initial candidate set. [Tauber et al., February 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9932893/)
- FIPAT/IFAA provides terminology authority; this does not verify mesh identity or geometry. Use controlled terminology and accepted synonyms. Its publications use CC BY-ND, so distribution access is not blanket permission to alter published tables. [IFAA terminology overview](https://ifaa.net/committees/anatomical-terminology-fipat/fipat-ifaa-terminologies/)
- The current OpenStax A&P 2e preface (publication date 2022-04-20) states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and additional commercial/AI permission language. Do not substitute older CC BY 4.0 mirror metadata for current terms. [Current preface](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/preface)
- Wisconsin displays CC BY 4.0 except where otherwise noted. Check individual images and embedded third-party material. [Lab 7](https://wisc.pb.unizin.org/humananatomylabmanual/chapter/__unknown__-7/)
- TTUHSC's bone table carries copyrighted-book and page notices against unauthorized reuse. Consult facts without copying its table. [Bone table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/bones_head_neck.html)
- No open-content reuse license was established for the inspected [Charles](https://anatomie.lf2.cuni.cz/en/skull) or [Loyola](https://www.meddean.luc.edu/lumen/meded/grossanatomy/h_n/hn_obj.html) pages.

Confidence is high for separating bones, features, spaces, and passages; moderate for proposed priority; unknown for current fine-feature adequacy, efficacy of the proposed sequence, and clinical accuracy. Research and source publication do not replace target-level anatomical review.
